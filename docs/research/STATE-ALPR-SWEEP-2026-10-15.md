# State ALPR sweep — the 23 states + DC not previously reviewed (2026-10-15)

**Not legal advice; secondary-source desk research, not reviewed by a lawyer.** Primary source: the
[recordinglaw.com 2026 state comparison](https://www.recordinglaw.com/us-laws/automated-license-plate-readers/alpr-laws-by-state/)
(dated Sept 2026; its own caveat: "no statute located" is not "no law", and local rules, contracts and policies are out of scope).
Cross-checked with [NCSL's ALPR statute page](https://www.ncsl.org/technology-and-communication/automated-license-plate-readers-state-statutes)
(older, Feb 2022 version seen). Washington checked in news coverage. **Statute text was not read** for any state here — verify before
relying on a row. Pennsylvania and a few other rows came from a page whose last ~140k characters were not read.

## Result: no general ban on private plate reading found in any of them

| Jurisdiction | Status | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Alaska, Delaware, Massachusetts, Michigan, Mississippi, Nevada, New York, Pennsylvania, Rhode Island, South Carolina, South Dakota, West Virginia, Wisconsin, Wyoming | No ALPR statute located | Bills pending in several (MA H.3755; MI HB 5492 would reach private entities; NY A563A would reach non-government users; SC S.447/H.4013; PA several). **Watch list.** |
| Arizona | Incidental | A.R.S. § 44-7953 (Laws 2026 ch. 232): law-enforcement contracts with camera businesses must bar selling data to third parties. Not a use ban. |
| District of Columbia | Incidental | D.C. Code § 50-2443 (agency traffic systems); MPD policy. |
| Hawaii | Incidental | HRS §§ 291J-5.5, 291L-4.5 (exception inside facial-recognition rules). |
| Indiana | **Narrow statute** | Ind. Code §§ 32-25.5-3.8-2 to -4 (HEA 1150, 2026): HOAs may not operate ALPRs unless installed by/for law enforcement. Not applicable to a feed viewer, but note it. |
| Louisiana | Incidental | La. R.S. 14:57.1 — criminal damage to crime cameras; no data rules. |
| Missouri | None in statute | Executive Order 26-18 (Sept 16, 2026) bars state-funded entities' vendors from commercialising Missouri data; SB 1027 pending. |
| North Dakota | Incidental | N.D.C.C. § 29-29.4-01 excludes readers from the drone chapter only. |
| Ohio | Incidental | R.C. 149.43(A)(1)(yy) public-records exclusion. |
| Oklahoma | Narrow statute | Okla. Stat. tit. 47 § 7-606.1 (uninsured-vehicle program); does not prohibit private use. |
| **Washington** | **Statute enacted** | SB 6002, the Driver Privacy Act, signed 2026-03-30 and reported effective immediately: 21-day deletion, sharing limits, sensitive-location limits, warrant needed for agencies to obtain private-entity ALPR data. Mostly regulates public agencies; **whether it reaches private operators was not established.** Read the enrolled text on leg.wa.gov. |

## Suggested follow-ups (for counsel, not code changes)

1. Washington: read SB 6002's definitions of "operator"/"ALPR system" and any private-use provision.
2. Indiana HEA 1150: confirm it is confined to HOAs.
3. Re-check the watch-list bills each legislative session (MI HB 5492, NY A563A, SC S.447).
4. California (already on the "reviewed" list) remains the only state with a private-operator duty regime in this set of findings.

No change to `JurisdictionPolicy`: nothing here supports adding a state to the off lists.
